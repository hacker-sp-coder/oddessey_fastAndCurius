# Evaluation Rubric Specification (Version 1.0.0)

## 1. Overview and Philosophy

The **PitchLens Viability Rubric** provides a standardized, explainable framework for triaging early-stage startup pitches. 

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Heuristic Disclaimer**: Scores generated by this system represent preliminary screening heuristics designed to help investors and accelerators triage pitches and identify diligence gaps. **A heuristic score is NOT a statistically validated probability or guarantee of commercial survival, product-market fit, or financial return.**

---

## 2. Weighted Dimensions (Total = 100 Points)

The rubric evaluates 7 fundamental criteria, each normalized to a 0–100 score. The overall composite score is calculated as:

$$\text{Overall Viability Score} = \sum_{i=1}^{7} \frac{\text{Criterion Score}_i \times \text{Weight}_i}{100}$$

| Dimension | Key | Weight | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Problem Clarity** | `problem_clarity` | **15%** | Severity, quantifiable magnitude, and customer pain urgency. |
| **Target Customer** | `target_customer` | **15%** | Specificity of buyer persona, commercial channel, and user segment. |
| **Market Evidence** | `market_evidence` | **15%** | Measured pilot metrics, retention signals, and customer demand evidence. |
| **Differentiation & Moat** | `differentiation` | **15%** | Defensibility, technical architecture, and structural switching moats. |
| **Technical Feasibility** | `feasibility` | **15%** | Realistic technology architecture and founder-market domain fit. |
| **Business Model** | `business_model` | **15%** | Monetization strategy, unit pricing clarity, and margin feasibility. |
| **Scalability & Growth** | `scalability` | **10%** | Capital-efficient distribution leverage, network effects, and roadmap. |
| **Total** | | **100%** | |

## 3. Scoring Breakdown per Dimension

### 3.1 Problem Clarity (Weight: 15%)
- **80–100 (High)**: Quantified financial or operational loss (e.g. "Farmers lose 25–40% annual crop yield", "clinics waste 3.2 hours/week"). Identifies structural failure in alternatives.
- **60–79 (Moderate)**: Clear qualitative pain points described, but lacking precise quantitative loss metrics or scale baseline.
- **0–59 (Low)**: Vague dissatisfaction, generic buzzwords, or "vitamin" rather than "painkiller" problem.

### 3.2 Target Customer (Weight: 15%)
- **80–100 (High)**: Specific commercial buyer and end-user defined (e.g. "Rural agricultural credit cooperatives licensing B2B2C for 3,400 smallholders").
- **60–79 (Moderate)**: Broad market definition (e.g. "small businesses" or "all hospitals") without specifying purchasing authority.
- **0–59 (Low)**: "Everyone with a smartphone" or undefined audience.

### 3.3 Market Evidence & Traction (Weight: 15%)
- **80–100 (High)**: Empirical numbers cited (e.g. "pilot with 3,400 active farmers, 78% monthly retention, 22% crop loss reduction").
- **60–79 (Moderate)**: Early user interviews, letters of intent (LOIs), or survey interest without measured retention or deployed usage.
- **0–59 (Low)**: Pure speculation with zero empirical validation signals.

### 3.4 Differentiation & Moat (Weight: 15%)
- **80–100 (High)**: Structural barrier cited (e.g. "Offline edge neural network running in under 400ms without cellular connectivity", proprietary dataset, or deep distribution exclusivity).
- **60–79 (Moderate)**: Incremental UX improvements or feature-level differences vulnerable to replication by incumbent APIs.
- **0–59 (Low)**: Generic wrapper around public third-party models with no defensible differentiation.

### 3.5 Technical & Operational Feasibility (Weight: 15%)
- **80–100 (High)**: Founder-market pedigree directly matches problem complexity (e.g. "Senior ML engineer paired with 14-year plant pathology scientist"). Realistic deployment constraints addressed.
- **60–79 (Moderate)**: Credible technical architecture outlined, but key domain hires or operational execution steps are unaddressed.
- **0–59 (Low)**: Unrealistic technical claims (e.g. "AGI with 100% accuracy on day one") or severe skill mismatch.

### 3.6 Business Model (Weight: 15%)
- **80–100 (High)**: Clear monetization logic, specific pricing ($1.20/farmer/year SaaS), and alignment with buyer ROI (reducing loan default risk).
- **60–79 (Moderate)**: General monetization direction stated (e.g. "subscription fee") without unit economics, pricing tiers, or payment collection channels.
- **0–59 (Low)**: "We will monetize later through ads or big data."

### 3.7 Scalability & Growth (Weight: 10%)
- **80–100 (High)**: Distribution engine leverages existing channel aggregators or partnerships to reach 50,000+ users capital-efficiently.
- **60–79 (Moderate)**: Growth relies on direct manual sales with linear headcount scaling.
- **0–59 (Low)**: No go-to-market strategy or distribution flywheel explained.

## 4. Evaluator Implementations

1. **Deterministic Evaluator**:
   - Executes offline with zero network or GPU requirements.
   - Applies transparent regex patterns, entity matching, and rule heuristics to extract verbatim evidence lines and calculate reproducible scores.
   - Produces diagnostic rule tags and flags missing evidence fields.
   - Identified in reports as `Deterministic Heuristic Rubric (Rule-Based Fallback)`.

2. **Groq LLM Evaluator**:
   - Uses configurable model (`llama-3.3-70b-versatile`).
   - Prompts for strict JSON matching the exact Pydantic `EvaluationResult` schema.
   - In the event of network timeouts, rate limits, or schema mismatches, automatically falls back to the deterministic evaluator with transparent diagnostic annotations.
